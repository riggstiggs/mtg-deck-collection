# Deck Guide: Korvold, Fae-Cursed King ("The Guest List")

## ⭐⭐ SEATING CHART pivot (2026-08-06, later same session) — READ THIS FIRST
The question "who is Korvold's co-host?" was the wrong question. The deck doesn't
need a second lead — it needs **a host, and then the court.** Korvold must stay
the ONLY dragon/apex predator in the room; everyone else is trying to survive
dinner, not compete with him.

**Vraska, Golgari Queen is IN — but as the Queen Mother, not a co-host.** {2}{B}{G},
$6.22. She is Lady Olenna Tyrell: not the monster, not the spectacle, the person
at the head table who already won before dinner started. She's the political
power everyone is trying to impress — quietly smiling while the room argues,
until her ultimate ("−9: whenever your creatures connect, that player loses")
lands as pure sovereign authority, not a fight. Chosen over Wasitora, Nekoru
Queen explicitly BECAUSE she isn't another predator — "two dragons" was
rejected as a worse story than "one dragon + the woman who was always in
control." Her +2 (sac-for-value) is a bonus, not the point; her narrative job
is presence and inevitability, not synergy.

**The hierarchy (seating chart), replaces "narrative roles":**
- **Tier 1 — The Host:** Korvold. The only apex predator. Unique.
- **Tier 2 — The Court:** Vraska (Queen Mother), + top nobility/socialites/aristocrats.
- **Tier 3 — The Guests:** visitors, celebrants, revelers (the "Guest" cards live here).
- **Tier 4 — The Staff:** ONE butler, ONE chef IF iconic enough, maybe one steward.
  NO redundancy — one pick per role, full stop.
- **Tier 5 — Entertainment:** musicians, dancers, jesters — hired for nobility.
- **Tier 6 — The Meal:** harvest, food, wine, dessert.
- **Tier 7 — Chaos:** poison/scandal/fight/the uninvited guest.

**New litmus test — sharper than "who's in the painting":** ***"Who receives an
invitation?"*** A butcher — no. A random farmer — no. A Duchess — absolutely. A
famous performer — yes. A visiting ambassador — yes. A social climber —
definitely. This is more restrictive than "could exist at a banquet" — it asks
whether this specific person would be ON THE GUEST LIST.

**Deck title:** confirmed as **"The Guest List"** (was briefly "Dinner Is
Served") — chosen because it provokes the exact question that IS the deck's
reveal: "wait... why is every card a person you'd invite to a banquet?"

## Design pivot #1 (2026-08-06, earlier same session)
The concept evolved mid-session from "Hospitality" to **The Guest List**. This
supersedes the original "banquet/hospitality" framing below where they conflict —
kept the original for the origin story, but the litmus test below is the live one.

**New litmus test:** *"If I painted one giant scene of this banquet, would this
person naturally be in the room?"* Not every card has to be literally seated at
the table, but every card needs a believable role within the event.

**Register: Game of Thrones / House of the Dragon / Bridgerton / vampire court.**
Polished nobility with underlying tension — "Red Wedding by way of a Bridgerton
ball." Explicitly NOT generic medieval life — no butcher shops, tavern kitchens,
farmhands, street merchants. Those exist in a medieval world but don't belong at
THIS event. (This is why the original "Hospitality" tavernkeeper/butcher/chef
picks were cut — right world, wrong venue.)

**Who's in the room:**
- **At the table:** nobles, aristocrats, socialites, honored guests, visitors,
  gluttons, political rivals, the person everyone is gossiping about, the guest
  who drank too much, the guest nobody remembers inviting.
- **Around the room:** butler, steward, head servant, a single chef IF iconic
  enough, entertainers — court musicians, ballroom dancers, jesters, performers
  hired specifically for nobility (not "random tavern bard").
- **The atmosphere:** dancing, toasts, gifts, fine wine, candles, decorations,
  ballroom, music, courtly conversation.

**The story arc:** begins elegant and refined → arguments begin → people become
greedy/gluttonous → politics turn dangerous → someone makes a scene → someone
shouldn't have been invited → Korvold stops being merely the host and becomes
the final course.

**Intrigue, done right:** NOT generic murder/assassins (already cut Hapatra,
Gumdrop Poisoner for this). Wanted instead: whispered gossip, political
maneuvering, poisoned wine, scandal, jealousy, duels of honor, rival houses,
subtle betrayals. Violence should feel like the **collapse of etiquette**, not
an action scene.

**Card test:** "I could picture this person walking into the ballroom" = strong
candidate. "This person works somewhere else in the city" = cut.

## Commander Strategy
**Korvold, Fae-Cursed King** {2}{B}{R}{G} — Legendary Creature — Dragon Noble, 4/4
*   **Archetype:** Jund sacrifice/Food value engine, theme-first
*   **Bracket:** TBD (target: legitimate synergistic deck, not cEDH — see Design Philosophy)
*   **Core Goal:** Tell the complete story of a grand fantasy banquet that spirals into
    chaos as Korvold consumes it. Every card should earn its slot on BOTH flavor and
    mechanical merit — sacrifice/Food/value engine feeding Korvold's counters + card
    draw, while the board tells a coherent narrative of hosts, guests, staff,
    entertainment, the meal itself, and the intrigue that unravels it.

Flying. Whenever Korvold enters or attacks, sacrifice another permanent. Whenever you
sacrifice a permanent, put a +1/+1 counter on Korvold and draw a card.

## Design Philosophy (Jamie's brief, 2026-08-06 — verbatim)

This deck is **theme first**. I am intentionally building around a narrative rather
than simply assembling the strongest Korvold list possible.

That said, I do **not** want this to be a weak "joke deck." The goal is to create a
deck that tells an incredible story while still being capable of holding its own in a
typical Commander pod. It doesn't need to compete with cEDH, but I would like it to be
a legitimate, synergistic deck that can win games through good gameplay rather than
novelty alone.

When possible, I want cards that satisfy both:
*   excellent thematic fit
*   strong mechanical synergy

### Core Theme — Hospitality
Imagine a grand medieval fantasy banquet. The deck should represent: preparing the
feast, gathering the harvest, welcoming guests, innkeepers and taverns, cooks and
kitchens, servants and butlers, nobles and socialites, entertainers, dancing,
celebrations, food and drink, gifts, hospitality, political intrigue, unexpected
chaos, and Korvold eventually consuming the celebration itself. The deck should feel
like an entire evening unfolding.

### Design Rule
When evaluating a card, ask: **"Could this card naturally exist during a grand
fantasy banquet?"** If the answer is yes, it deserves consideration.

### Narrative Roles
- **Preparation:** harvest, cooking, gathering ingredients, setting the table
- **Hosts:** innkeepers, celebrants, party organizers, nobility
- **Guests:** visitors, guests, socialites, revelers
- **Staff:** butlers, servants, attendants, stewards, security, kitchen staff
- **Entertainment:** musicians, dancers, jesters, performers, troupes
- **The Meal:** food, feasts, dessert, bread, pie, wine, hospitality
- **Intrigue:** poison, political maneuvering, unexpected guests, arguments, chaos
- **Finale:** Korvold ultimately consumes the feast

### Search scope
Do NOT limit recommendations to cards with the Food mechanic. Search broadly for
mechanics AND flavor matching: guest, visitor, host, hospitality, feast, banquet,
dinner, meal, harvest, kitchen, cook, chef, baker, butcher, innkeeper, steward,
servant, butler, attendant, celebrant, reveler, socialite, performer, musician,
dancer, jester, festival, party, dance, wine, toast, gift, offering, pie, bread,
cake, dessert, poison, court intrigue, nobility, ballroom, reception, tavern,
pantry, buffet, decorations, candles — anything that contributes to the atmosphere
of a grand fantasy feast.

### Scoring every candidate
Every recommendation gets two scores:
- **Flavor Score (1-10):** how strongly the card contributes to the banquet narrative
- **Mechanical Score (1-10):** how well the card supports Korvold's strategy

### Final Goal
Nobody should just say "nice Korvold Food deck." By the end of a game, the table
should recognize they weren't just playing against Korvold — they were attending a
lavish fantasy banquet that slowly descended into complete chaos before the host
devoured everything. That moment of discovery is the entire point of the deck.

### Origin story
This deck exists because Jamie found **Belligerent Guest** while filing cards for
her Manapool store, then discovered there are three "Guest"-named cards — all three
are locked-in inclusions (Belligerent Guest, Gluttonous Guest, Grand Ball Guest —
see Card Explanations). Jamie doesn't usually play BR, so Jund is a stretch outside
her usual Simic/green-anchored lane (see [[project_mtg_jamie_deck_identity]]); green
was added deliberately because Food shows up far more in green, and because Korvold
is BRG natively. Ramp is fair game if it earns its slot.

    Korvold to stay the ONLY apex predator in the room. "Two dragons" was
    explicitly judged a worse story than "one dragon + the woman who already won."

    her ability also duplicates Korvold's own sac-trigger rather than adding a
    new axis, unlike Vraska's ultimate.
